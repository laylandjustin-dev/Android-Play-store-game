A civilisation of pixels

Pixel Town

Every person is one pixel. Fifty-five settlers land on an island and start living — working, eating, pairing, ageing and dying — beside four rival civilisations running the same simulation, on the same map, under the same rules.

How to play

Spend ten points. Six traits, genetic to your people, for the whole run. Farming and Speed decide how much food you grow; Health how little you need, how well you survive a plague and how many children you have; Elements what winter costs you; Hunting what the wild gives and what your soldiers are worth; Logging how much timber you gather and how fast you build. No trait may open above 8, but every one of them can reach 20 in time.

Your people grow. Every ten years they earn a trait point, and the world stops until you spend it — the rival civilisations spend theirs the day they earn it, so nothing moves until everyone has. A new tech tier stops it the same way.

You do not give orders. The town elects a Premier each year who decides what gets built. You get influence — endorse a candidate, petition the sitting Premier, veto a build, or call an early election.

Every decade a generation comes of age and earns a trait point. It waits until you spend it. Reaching a tech tier stops the world until you choose what your scholars learned.

The run ends in collapse, conquest, endurance at three centuries, or ascension — tier six with four hundred people, which most runs never see.

This is the game, not a mock-up. Every tick, election and war comes from the same :sim Kotlin module the Android app is built on, compiled to JavaScript.
